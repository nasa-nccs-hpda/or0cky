Prepared by the driver session for G. Tamkin. Facts are from `BENCHMARK_V100_PROTOCOL.md` sections 6 and 7 (jobs 37941503, 37941514-16, Fortran runs b1-b3 on node gpu005, exclusive). The decision is the owner's.

## 3. What this says
* The earlier statement "3.5 s challenge met" holds as a statement about the cycle average at 3.3-3.9 s per step (noise 0.2 s). It does not mean JAX is faster than the Fortran: the Fortran cycle is 2.4 s on this node, so JAX is 1.5-1.6x slower per step and about 2.4x slower per day once the day-boundary recompile is counted (1.36x with the stall fix: 188 s against 138 s per steady day), and compile adds 733 s once.
* Radiation is 68% of the Fortran step (RADIA 1.62 s of 2.37 s per step) and is the SAME code in both runs. JAX cannot win on speed while radiation stays on the host: it would need the other 32% of the step (0.75 s) to fall below what the JAX non-radiation work plus the radiation hand-off costs (now about 2.1 s per step), i.e. about 3x faster, and that still gives no more than parity.
* The only lever that changes that is a device radiation (the SOCRATES port: 135-295 h estimate, standing rule says no).
* What the experiment does show: the ported physics (dynamics, clouds, surface, ocean, ice, daily updates) runs stably on a V100 for two days inside the ensemble spread at a loose margin, with a hybrid boundary that is explicit.

## 4. Options
A. **Stop here.** Deliverables stay: frozen benchmark, scripts, two-day hybrid result, relaxed/unrelaxed table, documented limits.
B. **Continue narrowly (recommended if the aim is a standalone multi-day JAX model, not speed):** the day-boundary fix is done (flag-gated); port the recorded inputs that block a longer run, cheapest first (sizing entry first); rerun the frozen test after each. No speed claim against Fortran.
C. **Continue for speed:** requires a device radiation (SOCRATES) or a different goal, e.g. throughput of many ensemble members on one GPU (NOT measured; no claim).

## 5. Limits of this evidence
One JAX run per configuration (three V100 nodes, noise about 0.2 s per step); Fortran timed on the same node type, 1 core, 3 runs; two days of one start date; GPU result not bitwise with CPU/Fortran; relaxed criterion chosen after seeing the results; recorded inputs mean JAX was never run independently of the real trajectory for land, vegetation and surface templates.

### How it is measured
Rerun the frozen job (`gpu/benchmark_v100.sh`, node type, restart, 54-step day, exclusive node, Fortran x3 beside it); report the held-step mean, the cycle average (4 held + 1 radiation, excluding step 0 and day-boundary steps, with `ROCKE_STAGE_ARGS=1` stated), the stage table from a `--timed` run, and the end-state score against the five members. A stage claim counts only if its stage seconds fall in the timed run AND the cycle average falls in the frozen run; noise is 0.2 s per step, so single savings below 0.2 s need repeated runs. Success for the speed track: cycle average at or below the Fortran's measured cycle average on the same node (2.37 s).

### Gate for the Ent + GHY step (decided from existing measurements, no new run)
The gate was "land chain on the V100 under about 0.3 s per step". GHY is already cheap on the device (the whole `surface_tiles_land` stage, with GHY batched in JAX and recorded Ent exports, is 0.037 s per step). Ent has no JAX version; the only computed-Ent path is the NumPy per-cell loop: GHY+Ent about 7.3 s per step on one core and 2.5-2.8 s on four workers (D175, JAX_COVERAGE_MATRIX rows 15 and the profile table). Against 0.3 s the gate FAILS for the existing path by 8x to 24x, so the remaining standalone work is a JAX port of Ent with data-dependent iteration counts, which the repo has never sized. Recommendation: stop the staged port here until you decide whether to fund an Ent port; nothing further is started.

## 9. Ent port (owner: "Ent port", 2026-10-11): step E1 done, the Ent export path in JAX
* `fullfidelity/ent_jax.py`: batched JAX version of the export path of `ent_ff.py` (clim_stats, photosynth_cond with the qsimp/trapzd canopy integration, Photosynth_analyticsoln, ci_cubic/rtsafe as a masked `lax.while_loop`, summarize_patch/entcell) over flat arrays of cells, patches and cohorts. * Cost on the V100 (`gpu_ent_bench.py`, job 37941525): 5.4 ms per Ent call for 1,146 cells (compile 7 s). One GHY substep makes up to 11 sub-iteration calls and a step has two substeps, so at most about 0.12 s per step in the worst case (typical 2-4 calls: 0.02-0.05 s). The compute part of the gate (under 0.3 s) is passed with margin.
* Not done: wiring into `ghy_jax.advnc_gdtm` (it already runs the GHY sub-iteration schedule on the device in a `lax.while_loop` and reads the Ent exports from recorded arrays; it needs the Ent call inside the loop body with the Ent state and Qf in the carry), the daily vegetation update at the boundary, the validation of the closed loop against the D171 closed-day records, and the template/rec changes (Ent exports and rows no longer recorded). ## 10. Ent port, steps E2-E4 done (2026-10-11): Ent computed inside the GHY loop of the assembled step (flag `ROCKE_ENT_LIVE=1`, default off)
* Wiring: `ghy_jax.advnc_gdtm(ent=...)` calls `ent_jax.ent_step` inside the sub-iteration `lax.while_loop` (after the time step is known, before `evap_limits`, as `ghy_ent_call`), with Qf carried (GHY.f:2621), the Ent state and constants riding in the carried land state (`dyn_next`), and the last sub-iteration's exports used for the final `evap_limits`. `jax_surface._land` / `_ghy_batch` plumb the inputs (recorded Ca, cosz1, vis_rad, dvis, Qf entry, end-of-day flag; with `ROCKE_RADCOLS=1` cosz1/vis/dvis are computed from our radiation state). The day-boundary prescribed LAI / albedo update (`ent_daily_ff`, host) runs once per day at the end-of-day step (`ent_daily` stage, 0.23 s for two boundaries).
* Closed-loop check on real records (`ent_live_check.py`, nov26 step 33312, all 753 land cells, start state of the record): GHY with live Ent against GHY with the RECORDED Ent exports: identical sub-iteration counts in all 753 cells; every GHY output within 3e-16 of scale (w, ht, tsns exactly equal).
* Assembled step, 3 steps on the CPU (flag on against off): 403 of 561 arrays bitwise equal at step 0; land GHY accumulators differ up to 1.8e-6 of scale. Reason (hypothesis, consistent with the standalone check being exact): `rtsafe` stops at `xacc` = 1e-4, so the photosynthesis result depends on the iteration path, and in the assembled step GHY receives OUR PBL outputs (differing from the real run's at rounding level), which can change that path; the Fortran has the same sensitivity. Later steps differ by trajectory divergence, as for any rounding-level perturbation.
* Two-day V100 run (job 37941526; `ROCKE_ENT_LIVE=1 ROCKE_RADCOLS=1 ROCKE_STAGE_ARGS=1`, server radiation): 108 of 108 steps finite; cold step 0 814 s (compile), step 1 99.6 s (recompile for the carried Ent state), held steps 1.84 s, radiation steps 10.44 s, cycle average 3.56 s (the same as without live Ent: Ent cost is below the noise), steps 48 and 96 without stall (1.9 s, 2.0 s), total 1,290 s. Scores: day 1 worst ratio at steps >= 3 QCI 2.37 at step 3 (the same value as every earlier run: it is set by the early realisation), QCL 1.72, day 2 never beyond 2x (worst QCI 1.17); unrelaxed NOT MET, relaxed MET.
* What is still recorded after this (declared): the SURFACE template rows other than the radiation columns (PBL template columns, tile rows and sets, soil static columns, irrigation), the per-call Ent exports ws_can, shc_can, fv, height, albedo (recomputing them from the updated Ent cells after the daily update is not done; between daily updates they are constant), the CONDSE entry set, the first-call Qf (record), the land forcing columns and nit_rebuild rows. The run is therefore still not standalone. Ent itself (photosynthesis, canopy conductance, 10-day climate statistics, daily LAI) is computed.
