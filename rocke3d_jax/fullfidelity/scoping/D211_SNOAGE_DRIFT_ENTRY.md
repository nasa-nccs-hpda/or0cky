## 1. Answer
The SNOAGE update in our chain is correct. The drift is caused by differences in the precipitation (amount and rain/snow phase) that our CONDSE computes from OUR atmospheric state, which differs from the real state by far more than rounding from step 1 on (T up to 5e-4, Q up to 5e-2 relative to the column maximum in the worst column at step 1; step 0 is 1e-14). Because SNOAGE is multiplied by exp(-PREC) only where EPREC < 0 (snow), and the phase decision is discontinuous, a small state difference turns into a jump of SNOAGE in cells that rain in the real model and snow in ours (or the reverse). No candidate fix exists to propose: there is no defect to patch (section 5).

## 5. Proposed fix
None. There is no defect in the SNOAGE update or its carry (items 1-4); a patch that forced our SNOAGE to follow the record would use the record as input (the D208 record-fed boundary does exactly that and hides the drift) and is not a fix of the chain. The drift can only shrink if the atmospheric state (precipitation) tracks the real one more closely. For a day that depends on the snow age (computed radiation), the practical options are: (a) accept that snow ages follow the chain's own precipitation (the same freedom the rest of the free-running atmosphere has), (b) keep the record-fed SNOAGE at the daily boundary for scored comparisons. This is a decision for the owner; no code was written for either.
