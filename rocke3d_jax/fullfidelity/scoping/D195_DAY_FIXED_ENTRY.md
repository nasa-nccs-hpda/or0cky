## 1. What was merged (task 1)
- `jax_surface.build_template` now also returns in `host` the recorded GHY rows (`nit_rows`) and the cells with recorded ffnit > 11 (`nit_hit`); new `jax_surface.nit_rebuild(tpl, host, prec, eprec, precss, strict=True)`: only when such a cell exists, ONE `jax.device_get` of PREC/EPREC/PRECSS (3 small arrays), `build_batch` of those rows with OUR precipitation (columns 143-145 as `surface_loop.Loop.stage_surface`), device replacement of `edts/ecnc/elai/ebet/nsub/dt` of exactly those cells (same shapes, no extra compile). Otherwise a no-op, no transfer. This is the code of `d194_nit_fix._fix` moved into the module; `d194_nit_fix.py`/`d194_run.py` are untouched (do not use them together with the merged option: it would apply the rule twice, same result).
- `Coupled(date, ..., nit_fix=True, nit_strict=True)`: the rule is ON by default; a new stage `nit_rebuild` (kind REC/NP, in `STAGES2`) sits between `tile_mask_check` and `template_apply`, so it is in the non-JAX list and in the stage table with its time; `info['nit_rebuild']` = cells and number of device->host arrays per step. `nit_fix=False` gives the D193 behaviour.
- `nit_strict=True` (default) keeps the assertion `nit == ffnit` of `build_batch_nit` NOT caught, as in the NumPy reference. `nit_strict=False` (explicit option, used only for the day below) uses `jax_surface._build_batch_nit_report`: a copy of `build_batch_nit` in which the assertion is REPORTED in `jax_surface.NIT_MISMATCH` instead of raised (one difference besides: the dts row of such a cell is zeroed before it is refilled; identical when the count matches). The width assertion (batch wider than the compiled `max_substeps`) is kept.
- Transfer declaration: the read is one `jax.device_get` call of 3 arrays (1.1 MB total scale: 3 x 72 x 46 float64 = 80 KB), done on the 13 steps with an ffnit>11 cell (steps 7, 8, 9, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27; 15 cell-substeps, see `d195_results/rebuild_cause.txt`), none on the other 41. The `d191_run.py` guarded step-0 run has no such cell. Measured cost of the stage over the day: 2.9 s (54 calls).
- Test `tests/test_jax_surface.py::test_nit_rule_ffnit_gt_11_uses_our_precipitation` (real nov26_day step 7 record, cell (64,19), ffnit 12): (1) with grid precipitation equal to the record the schedule equals the recorded-row schedule (atol 1e-9); (2) with a changed precipitation `edts` and `nsub` of the ffnit>11 cell are bit-for-bit those of the NumPy `build_batch` of the row with our precipitation, differ from the recorded-precipitation schedule (non-vacuity), and every other cell keeps its recorded schedule; (2b) the non-strict path gives the same schedule when the count matches, with no mismatch reported; (3) no ffnit>11 cell: the template object is returned unchanged and no read happens. ## 5. Tile-mask mismatches (diagnosed, not fixed)
Mismatches of the ocean/lake-water type: **steps 48 (54 cells), 49, 50, 51 (3 each), 52, 53 (4 each)**; ice type 0; none at steps 0-47 (D193 had 1 cell at step 43 on its different trajectory; with the nit rule step 43 is clean). Diagnosis from the records (cheap part, state not available because per-step arrays were deleted):
- All mismatching slots are cells where the REAL record of step 48 (it 33360, the day boundary) has an ocean-type tile with a tiny open-water fraction (1.2e-8 .. 8.8e-5) in cells that were pure ice-type at the step before (all 54 cells have an ice tile at 33359 and 33360, latitude rows j = 32, 38-43); our MELT_SI result has no open-water tile there.
- 51 of the 54 are gone again in the record of step 49; the 3 that persist ((54,41), (64,39), (71,39); fractions 8.8e-5, 1.7e-5, 7.1e-6) are the 3 mismatches of steps 49-51; the record's type-1 cell set also changes by single cells at 33363-33365 (+1, -1, +1/-1); I did not map these to the 4 mismatches of steps 52-53.
- So this is a difference of the SEA-ICE FRACTION (rsi) at the day boundary (the real model has rsi slightly below 1 in 54 cells at that step), appearing exactly where the day-boundary daily updates, which are not applied in this run (as in D157/D171), would act. It is **not** shown to be the new-ice-tile donor rule: the template of every step is the recorded one, so a tile that exists in the record has its columns, and the mismatch is the opposite case (record has the tile, we do not). I did NOT implement the donor rule (D190 section 6): it is not small and could not be verified against the reference here (the reference chain has the same record-derived template). Hypothesis only, not tested: a daily ocean/ice update at it 33360.
- Effect on the score: not isolated. Q is near from step 41 (before the mismatches start), so the Q near steps are not explained by them.
